What is an ICSR?

An Individual Case Safety Report is the record of one patient's suspected adverse reaction. It is the basic unit of pharmacovigilance and the input for signal detection like Project 1.

Four minimum criteria

Identifiable patient, identifiable reporter, at least one suspect drug, at least one adverse event. Without all four the case is not valid for reporting, but you should follow up.

Seriousness vs severity

Seriousness is about outcome or regulatory criteria (death, life-threatening, hospitalisation, disability, congenital anomaly, medically important). Severity is intensity, such as mild or severe. A mild event can be serious; a severe headache may not be.

Expectedness

An event is expected if listed in the reference document (label/SmPC, or the Investigator's Brochure in trials). Serious and unexpected is the priority combination.

Day 0 and deadlines

The clock starts when the company first receives valid information. Post-marketing serious cases: 15 calendar days. Trial SUSARs (serious, related, unexpected): 7 days if fatal or life-threatening, else 15. Non-serious post-marketing cases go into periodic reports (PBRER/PSUR).

Naranjo scale

Ten weighted questions scoring how likely a drug caused the event. It is a structured aid, not proof. WHO-UMC is another common scale.

MedDRA coding

Reported terms like "swollen face" are coded to standard MedDRA terms (for example Angioedema) so cases can be grouped and analysed. Signal ↔ case processing: Project 1 finds a statistical signal; the supporting ICSRs are then reviewed one by one here, and the aggregated results go back as evidence for signal assessment. Duplicates, follow-up, QC and audit trail

Duplicates are the same event reported twice, found by matching patient, drug, event, dates and reporter. Follow-up asks the reporter for missing details. QC is a second-person check before submission. The audit trail records who changed what and when, which proves traceability.
